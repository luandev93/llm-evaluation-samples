# Evaluation Criteria

Use a 1–5 scale for each criterion.

## General Scale

- **1 — Major problems:** The response fails the criterion in an important way.
- **2 — Significant problems:** The response has clear weaknesses that materially reduce quality.
- **3 — Acceptable but flawed:** The response is usable but has noticeable issues.
- **4 — Good:** The response satisfies the criterion with only minor issues.
- **5 — Excellent:** The response fully satisfies the criterion.

## Criteria

### Accuracy

Are factual claims correct and supported by the information available?

### Instruction Following

Does the response follow the explicit and implicit instructions in the prompt?

### Relevance

Does the response stay focused on the user's request without unnecessary digressions?

### Completeness

Does the response address all important parts of the request?

### Clarity

Is the response easy to understand, well organized, and appropriate for the intended audience?

### No Unsupported Claims

Does the response avoid inventing facts, sources, capabilities, or details that are not supported?

For this criterion:

- **5** = No unsupported claims
- **1** = Serious unsupported or fabricated claims

### Overall Quality

Considering all criteria together, how well does the response satisfy the user?

## Preference Decision

The preferred answer should be selected based on the rubric and the user's instructions, not on length, confidence, or writing style alone.

A longer answer is not automatically better. A response that follows the instructions precisely may be preferable to a more detailed response that violates explicit constraints.
